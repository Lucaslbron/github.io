4 . Conclusion We can see that in the surface plot, the outlet for the R134a is -9.81 F. I initially thought this was an error, but after some research this actually seems to be intended. The localized minimum temperature reading of -9.81 F at the outlet is caused by rapid expansion cooling (flashing) resulting from an unpressurized exit boundary condition. Liquid R-134a at an inlet temperature of 40 F requires a saturation pressure of approximately 49.7 psia to remain in the liquid phase without vaporizing. Discharging into standard atmospheric pressure (14.7 psia or 1 atm) forces the refrigerant's saturation temperature down toward around -15 F, triggering localized phase-change cooling as pressure drops near the exit. Adjusting the outlet boundary condition to reflect a realistic pressurized loop 50 - 60 psia would eliminate this expansion artifact and would stabilize the liquid flow domain.
